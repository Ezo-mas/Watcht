# M4: Tracking parity — historical decisions and implementation notes

This preserves the milestone notes as recorded before they were moved out of the plan. Their branch and review statuses are historical; see the [rewrite plan](../rewrite-plan.md) for current status.

- **Status: in progress**, in slices: (1) the `watch_events` table and `internal/tracking` (recording, dedupe, merging, removal), Go only; (2) the tracking API, the detail pages' watch actions, and `/history`; then a UI pass that brings the existing pages (header, search, movie and show pages) to the look the maintainer wants and updates `DESIGN.md`, so later slices build on it (maintainer decision, 2026-11-04); (3) watchlist and ratings, from tables to pages; (4) `user_settings` (time zone), show progress, and the settings page; (5) the dashboard with its widgets, saved layout, and day-based stats, the phone tab bar, and the first Playwright tests; (6) local library search with FTS5. The legacy app's recommendations and social feed blocks aren't ported (social features are a non-goal).
  - Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-03):
    - Removing a watch from history also removes the events merged into it: they were the same watch. A media server re-sending the same `source_event_id` later records a new watch.
    - Marking a season or show watched adds a watch for each aired episode that isn't watched yet, skipping unaired ones and missing or not-found episodes and seasons.
    - Marking something watched offers "just now", the release or air date, or another date and time picked in the user's time zone.
  - Slice 1 is merged (#28): migration 00008 (`watch_events`) and `internal/tracking` with `Record` and `Remove`. Nothing calls it yet; slice 2's API is the first user. Decisions made while building it:
    - A repeated watch is stored with `merged_into_id` pointing at the kept watch, which is always a kept row, never another merged one. A new event merges into the nearest kept watch of the same item within the window; equally close ones go to the one recorded first. Merged events don't extend the window. The window is an option (default 3 hours), not a setting yet.
    - `watch_events.id` is `AUTOINCREMENT`, like `users` and `api_tokens`: the SPA will hold event IDs, and a stale remove must not hit a newer watch. `Remove` takes any event of a watch, kept or merged, and deletes the whole watch.
    - Manual marks have no `source_event_id`; every other source must send one. The `source` CHECK already lists `jellyfin`, `plex`, `trakt_import`, and `letterboxd_import`, because widening a CHECK after v0.1.0 means rebuilding the table. A source not on the list (an API or script source, Emby) still needs that rebuild.
    - Watch times are stored to the second. A time more than 5 minutes past the server's clock is refused; years 1 to 9999 are accepted, so a film from the 1890s can be marked at its release date.
    - The completion rule (85% or marked played) arrives with the webhooks in M7, applied inside `tracking.Record`, not in the handler. Today's sources are completed watches by definition.
    - `Record` runs in one write transaction, and its core takes the caller's transaction, so slice 2's season marking (check what's watched, then insert) and M5's imports can be atomic.
  - Slice 2 is in review (branch `m4-tracking-api`): marking and history in `internal/tracking` (`mark.go`, `read.go`), the routes in `internal/httpapi/tracking.go`, the SPA's mark actions on the movie and show pages, and `/history`. Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-03):
    - A watch at the release or air date is stored as that date (`watched_on`, migration 00009, which also marks the earlier release and air date marks as dates) and shown and grouped by it in every time zone; `watched_at` is noon UTC on it, only so it sorts and dedupes with timed watches (maintainer decision, 2026-11-05; at first only noon UTC was stored). "Aired" means noon UTC on the air date has passed; an episode with no air date hasn't aired.
    - Until slice 4 adds the time zone setting, the "other date" picker and history's day headings use the browser's time zone.
    - A single episode can always be marked "just now" or at a picked time, aired or not, and with no air date; only "air date" needs a date that has passed. Season and show marks still skip unaired episodes.
    - Episodes are written `S01E02` everywhere.
    - `/history` is a grid of poster cards under day headings, after classic Trakt's history page: an episode shows its show's poster, the card's watched check removes the watch (after a confirm), a watchlist button is shown disabled until slice 3, a heart shows TMDB's rating, and each day shows its total watch time. It loads more as you scroll instead of using numbered pages.
  - Decisions made while building it:
    - Routes: `POST /movies/{id}/watches`, `POST /episodes/{id}/watches` (TMDB episode ID), `POST /shows/{id}/seasons/{n}/watches`, `POST /shows/{id}/watches`, `DELETE /watches/{id}`, `GET /movies/{id}/watches`, `GET /episodes/{id}/watches` (added in UI pass part 2), `GET /shows/{id}/watched` (each entry carries its `season_number`), `GET /history`. A mark's body is empty or `{}` (now), `{"watched_at": "<RFC 3339>"}`, or `{"release_date": true}`, never both; `release_date` is only ever `true` (maintainer decision, 2026-11-05). Watches carry `watched_on`, the date of a date-only watch or null. They work without the TMDB key, and only on stored titles (the page stores them on open).
    - Errors: 404 `not_found`, 400 `invalid_json` for a body that isn't JSON or has unknown fields (with a fixed message, so the decoder's text never echoes input), 400 `invalid_request` for valid JSON with bad values, 422 `no_release_date` / `not_released`, and 409 `episodes_pending` when a season or show mark comes before the background sync stored its episodes, so a mark never silently covers only part of a show.
    - Marking a show skips specials (season 0), which are marked as their own season. If two listed seasons share a number, show and season marks both use the one with the lowest TMDB ID, as the page does. A season or show mark checks what's watched and inserts in one write transaction.
    - History pages by a cursor, `<unix seconds>_<watch id>` (seconds may be negative, for watches before 1970), over kept watches, newest first. 50 per page by default, at most 100. A watch time that truncates to Go's zero time is refused. Each entry carries the episode's `air_date`, the title's or episode's `runtime` (minutes, 0 when unknown) and TMDB `vote_average` for the cards; the day totals are summed in the browser, since day boundaries depend on the time zone.
    - Watch removal is available from history, title-page plays menus, and episode and season cards (extended in UI pass part 2).

  - UI pass part 2 (branch `ui-show-pages`): the show page redesign and new season and episode pages, from maintainer screenshots (2026-11-09). Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-09):
    - Season and episode pages are routes with numbers in the URL: `/shows/{id}/seasons/{n}` and `/shows/{id}/seasons/{n}/episodes/{e}`. The episode page finds the episode in its stored season; marks still use the TMDB episode ID. The show page's `?season=N` goes away (old links redirect to the season page).
    - The show page's Seasons section is a poster grid with each season's watched progress, linking to the season pages. Episodes are listed, and a season marked, only on the season page.
    - Up Next and Recently Aired on the show page are placeholders until slice 4's server-side progress fills them, so the logic isn't written twice.
    - Show and season pages: a sage progress button ("57% watched", episodes watched of total, plays) with a progress bar under it; its + marks the rest with the when-choice, and its main part opens a menu (View history and confirmed Remove all plays). The episode page gets the movie's plays button, backed by a new `GET /api/v1/episodes/{id}/watches`. Watchlist, rating, and where-to-watch placeholders sit below, as on the movie page.
    - For slice 4 (from the review of part 2): the show page's and season grid's progress is counted in the browser for now: watched episodes by `season_number` against TMDB's `episode_count`. Watched episodes TMDB no longer lists still count, so the grid can read 100% while the season page (listed episodes only) shows less, and unaired episodes count in the total, so an airing show never reaches 100%. Slice 4's server-side progress should count listed episodes only and settle with the maintainer whether the total is listed or aired episodes (Trakt uses aired). The same gap affects Remove all plays on a season card: it deletes the visible season row's plays, so watches of episodes under a replaced or duplicate season row stay and the card still counts them; and a card whose season hasn't aired can't open its menu even when it has plays (the season page's progress button still removes them).
    - Maintainer review of part 2 (2026-11-09), changes in the same PR: a taller, full-colour hero with no left scrim or dimming, only a short fade behind the title and ratings row (maintainer decision); previous/next arrows on the hero of season and episode pages; the episode page's hero shows the episode's still (a large size from the API), season pages keep the show's backdrop. Season counts read "7 Seasons" (count first, specials not counted) in the section list and as the grid's heading; a season card's tooltip names the show and season ("Star Wars: The Clone Wars – Season 1"). Episode card bars run edge to edge with the card's bottom corners; nothing overlaps the scrollbar. Sort, a filter field, and a hide-watched (eye) toggle as placeholders over the season grid and episode lists. An "All episodes" view at `/shows/{id}/seasons/all` lists every episode, specials included, from a new `GET /api/v1/shows/{id}/episodes` (one query, not one request per season; maintainer decision). Season cards get a mark button that marks the season; since marking only adds aired episodes not yet watched, it also serves as "watch the remaining episodes" for a partly watched season. The show and season progress button's menu gets "Watch remaining" too, with "Rewatch" and "Drop" as placeholders (see the Backlog), as in Trakt's menus.
    - Remove all plays built in this pass (2026-11-10): authenticated `DELETE` counterparts of all four mark routes return `{"removed": n}` (kept plays, not merged source events). Each deletion is scoped to the user and runs in one write transaction; merged source events cascade with their kept play. Unknown stored titles return 404; empty history returns 0. A season resolves the same visible, lowest-TMDB-ID season as marking and removes its stored episodes by identity, including missing metadata; show removal covers every stored season, including specials and hidden seasons. Removal works while episode metadata is still pending.
    - Every Remove all plays menu action now uses shared `RemoveAllPlays` and `InlineConfirm`, then refreshes the relevant history/progress and toasts the removed count. Fully watched season checks are buttons opening the same removal menu as episode cards; partial seasons lead with Watch remaining, followed by air dates, other date, and confirmed removal. Open card menus raise their containing card above sibling bars. Watch again on completed season cards remains disabled pending the maintainer's decision: existing season marks intentionally skip watched episodes, so an explicit rewatch option is needed.
  - For later milestones (from the slice 1 review):
    - M7: `source_event_id` is unique per user and source, so with more than one Jellyfin or Plex server the ID needs the server's ID in it. A repeated ID naming a different item is returned as a duplicate of the stored event. The webhook handler must cap the payload size and strip any token before calling `Record`.
    - M5: re-running an import after removing an imported watch brings that watch back, since removal frees its `source_event_id`.
    - M9: Letterboxd dates have no time. Record them with `Event.DateOnly`, so they're stored as dates; at noon UTC they won't dedupe against a timed play from the same evening. Two things to settle first (from the slice 2 follow-up review): `DateOnly` takes the UTC date of `WatchedAt`, so pass the diary date at noon UTC rather than parsing it in the user's zone; and the future check compares noon UTC on that date with now, so an entry for today is refused before 12:00 UTC (and east of UTC+12, an entry for the local today can be refused all day). A plain date field on `Event`, with a date-aware future check, is likely the better API.
